# Interview Synthesis — Retention Across the User Journey

**Source:** 3 user interviews (Priya S. — 18mo power user; Tom R. — churned at 5 weeks; Amara L. — 10 days in), provided 2026-07-16.

**Caveat (flag before reading themes as fact):** n=3, qualitative, one interview per journey stage. Treat these as directional hypotheses to validate against broader data, not confirmed findings.

## Top 5 Themes

### 1. The app goes static after initial setup
Nothing visibly changes once the user has connected accounts and seen the first breakdown.
- Tom: "The app just showed me the same dashboard every time."
- Amara: "I check it occasionally but nothing has changed since the first day."

### 2. Users don't know what to do with the information they're given
The data is presented, but no next action is suggested.
- Amara: "I have been waiting for Nudge to tell me the next step."
- Tom: "I kept waiting for it to give me something to act on and it never really did."

### 3. Personalized pattern-surfacing is the strongest retention driver — when it happens
The moment Nudge revealed a habit the user hadn't consciously noticed is what created lasting engagement.
- Priya: "it started surfacing patterns I had not consciously registered — like that I always overspend in the last week of the month."
- Priya: "I feel like Nudge actually knows my financial habits now."

### 4. Time-to-value likely exceeds most users' patience window
The payoff that hooked Priya took months; other users don't stay that long.
- Priya: "it took about three months to get there and I think most people give up before that."
- Tom: "But after the initial setup it felt like nothing changed." (churned at week 5 — well short of three months)

### 5. Users want the app to actively demand something of them, not just display data
Passive dashboards read as inert; users compare unfavorably to tools that prompt action.
- Tom: "I switched to YNAB because at least that feels like it is asking something of me."
- Amara: "I check it occasionally but nothing has changed since the first day." (passive checking behavior, no pull back)

## Contradictions & Tensions

- **Setup completion ≠ perceived value.** Tom did everything asked of him — connected three accounts, set up a budget — and still experienced the app as static. Completing onboarding steps doesn't guarantee the user feels progress.
- **Strong first impression doesn't predict continued engagement.** Amara called the spending breakdown "shocking in a useful way," a genuinely strong aha moment — but by day 10 she's already down to occasional, passive checking. The initial hook and sustained engagement appear to be separate problems.
- **The retention mechanism that works (Priya's) may not be reachable in time for most users.** Priya's own words acknowledge this directly: the pattern-recognition payoff that made Nudge stick took ~3 months, and by her own estimate "most people give up before that" — consistent with Tom's actual 5-week churn.
- **Possible mismatch in what "engagement" means to different users.** Priya describes a passive ritual (Nudge surfaces insight to her, weekly). Tom wants the app to actively ask something of him. A single feature (e.g., a passive weekly summary) may satisfy Priya's mode but not address Tom's.

## Single Most Important Insight for Week-1 Retention

Nudge's real, validated retention driver — personalized pattern recognition — doesn't arrive until roughly three months in, per Priya. But churn data shows users don't wait that long (Tom left at week 5; Amara is already stalling at day 10, saying almost verbatim what Tom said before he left: *"I have been waiting for Nudge to tell me the next step"* vs. Tom's *"I kept waiting for it to give me something to act on."*). This means the authentic value driver cannot be the week-1 retention solution — it arrives too late. The task isn't to accelerate the real pattern-recognition insight (which needs data volume over time to be genuine), but to manufacture a credible, actionable "next step" immediately after the initial breakdown, to bridge the gap between day 1 and whenever real personalization kicks in. Amara, still active at day 10, is the person this needs to reach before she becomes Tom.
