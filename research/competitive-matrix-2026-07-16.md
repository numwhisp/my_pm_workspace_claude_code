# Competitive Matrix — Spending Insights & Budgeting Apps

**Scope:** Apps built around post-connection spending insights, budgeting, and financial habit building. Excludes primarily-banking, investing, and credit-monitoring products (e.g., excluded: Empower Personal Dashboard — investing-led; Credit Karma — credit-led; neobanks).

**Method:** Web research, July 2026. All facts below are sourced and linked; anything not directly sourced is marked as inference.

## 1. YNAB (You Need A Budget)

- **Core features:** Zero-based budgeting (assign every dollar a job), goal-setting/targets, debt payoff calculator, bank sync.
- **Pricing:** $14.99/mo or $109/yr ($9.08/mo effective). Single tier, no feature paywalls. 34-day free trial; free year for students. ([YNAB Pricing](https://www.ynab.com/pricing))
- **Target customer (inference):** Committed, methodology-driven budgeters actively trying to break paycheck-to-paycheck habits or pay down debt — willing to pay a premium for a disciplined system, not a passive dashboard.
- **Post-connection engagement:** Live push notifications to flag overspending in real time; a "Reflect" tab with a new Income vs. Spending insight that pulls up to 3 months of history for new users "from day one"; recurring-transaction icons; balance-hiding toggle for sharing. Engagement is largely opt-in/on-demand — driven by the budgeting methodology itself, not proactive personalized pushes. ([YNAB Live Push Notifications](https://www.ynab.com/blog/introducing-live-push-notifications), [YNAB What's New](https://www.ynab.com/whats-new))
- **Notable recent changes:** Live Push Notifications (new); Income vs. Spending insight added to Reflect tab.

## 2. Rocket Money

- **Core features:** Subscription detection/cancellation, bill negotiation, budgeting, credit score tracking.
- **Pricing:** Free tier (subscription tracking, basic budgeting, credit score). Premium is "pay what you think is fair," typically $7–$14/mo, 7-day free trial. Bill negotiation charges a 35–60% success fee on savings achieved. ([Rocket Money Pricing](https://help.rocketmoney.com/en/articles/2217739-how-much-does-rocket-money-cost))
- **Target customer (inference):** Mainstream, price-sensitive users primarily motivated by cutting recurring costs — lower budgeting-methodology commitment than YNAB, freemium-first.
- **Post-connection engagement:** iOS widgets showing upcoming bills/payday/spending; proactive money-saving actions (subscription cancellation, bill negotiation) that create recurring reasons to check in; credit score tracking.
- **Notable recent changes:** New web/desktop app access for Premium members; unlimited custom budget categories for Premium; new iOS widgets. ([Rocket Money Premium Features](https://help.rocketmoney.com/en/articles/2677184-premium-membership-features))

## 3. Copilot Money

- **Core features:** AI-powered transaction categorization, investment + net worth tracking bundled in (no extra tier), subscription/recurring detection, deep Apple ecosystem integration (Shortcuts, Widgets, Wallet).
- **Pricing:** $13/mo or $95/yr. 1-month free trial via App Store. ([Copilot Pricing](https://copilot.money/pricing/))
- **Target customer (inference):** Design-conscious Apple-ecosystem users wanting one polished view of spending, investments, and net worth — historically iOS-only, web access only added December 2025 (still limited as of March 2026).
- **Post-connection engagement:** Recurring/subscription detection view; Apple Widgets on the home screen; a beta "Money Assistant" for insights/long-term planning (not yet fully rolled out); building an MCP (Model Context Protocol) integration so users can ask outside AI tools about their spending without opening the app.
- **Notable recent changes:** Added cryptocurrency tracking; began building MCP integration with external AI tools (May 2026); beta Money Assistant; improved web transaction splitting/filtering. ([Copilot Money Release Notes](https://releasebot.io/updates/copilot-money))

## 4. Monarch Money

- **Core features:** Unified dashboard across accounts/investments/loans, shared "Household" access for couples, AI Assistant, Flex budgeting, cash flow projection, connects to 13,000 institutions.
- **Pricing:** Core plan $14.99/mo or $99.99/yr; Plus plan $199/yr (adds advanced forecasting, business/rental income tracking, Morningstar-powered investment analysis). No free tier; 7-day free trial. ([Monarch Pricing](https://www.monarch.com/pricing))
- **Target customer (inference):** Couples/households and former Mint users wanting an all-in-one net-worth + budgeting dashboard; Plus tier targets self-described "optimizers."
- **Post-connection engagement:** AI Assistant delivers weekly financial summaries, surfaces trends, and answers natural-language questions using the user's actual data; goals were redesigned in 2026 into two distinct experiences (save-up goals vs. debt-paydown goals); receipt scanning; retail extension.
- **Notable recent changes:** New AI Assistant (weekly summaries + natural-language Q&A) as part of the 2026 "Winter Release"; Goals redesign; Monarch states user data isn't used to train external models. ([Monarch AI Features](https://help.monarch.com/hc/en-us/articles/37526856682260-AI-in-Monarch), [Monarch Winter Release](https://www.monarch.com/blog/winter-release))

## 5. PocketGuard

- **Core features:** Signature "In My Pocket" number (safe-to-spend after bills/savings), subscription detection, debt payoff plan, bill tracking.
- **Pricing:** Limited free tier (2 accounts, 2 categories). Plus: $12.99/mo, $74.99/yr, or $149.99 one-time lifetime. ([PocketGuard Pricing](https://pocketguard.com/pricing/))
- **Target customer (inference):** Users who want one simple, recurring "can I spend this?" number rather than full budgeting complexity — likely more financially anxious or budgeting-averse than YNAB's audience.
- **Post-connection engagement:** The "In My Pocket" number itself functions as a recurring check-in metric; a new "Pace" feature (Plus, iPhone) shows spending pace vs. remaining days/budget for the month; push notifications for bill due dates and large transactions (Plus).
- **Notable recent changes:** Apple Card/Cash/Savings integration (March 2026); new "Pace" feature; ability to review prior months' budgets; fixed a long-standing double-counting bug. ([PocketGuard News](https://pocketguard.com/news/))

## Mint (Discontinued — Historical Context Only)

Not a live competitor — Intuit discontinued Mint, directing users to Credit Karma (per source, shutdown was announced for January 1, 2024). Included here only because several current competitors (notably Monarch) position themselves as Mint's successor, and its engagement model is instructive as a predecessor.

- **Notification/summary system while live:** A weekly retention-focused push notification, delivered Sundays, linking to a weekly summary page showing top spending categories, where the user made the most purchases, and which days they spent the most. Users could also opt into daily, weekly, or monthly notification cadence. ([Mint Weekly Summary — Taplytics](https://taplytics.com/blog/mint-weekly-review-summary-notification/), [Mint Budget Alerts](https://mint.intuit.com/how-mint-works/alerts))
- **Other alerts:** Over-budget alerts, large-transaction alerts, ATM fee alerts, bill due-date reminders. Mint briefly offered direct Bill Pay but discontinued that specific feature in May 2018 while keeping bill tracking/reminders. ([Mint Feature History](https://cashandcoffeeclub.com/mint-by-intuit-budget-app/))
- **Relevance to Engage v2:** Mint's weekly summary was purely descriptive (top categories, top spending days) — the same "shows what happened, not what to do" pattern users are still complaining about today (see Gap 1 below). This suggests the gap has persisted across at least one full generation of this product category, not just among current players.

## White Space for Engage v2

**Caveat:** these are inferred from public feature descriptions and reviews, not hands-on competitive testing — treat as directional, worth validating before committing scope.

1. **No competitor (live or historical) pushes a single, ranked, personalized "top insight + one next action" without the user asking for it.** YNAB's Reflect insights and Copilot's Money Assistant are both opt-in/on-demand. Monarch's AI Assistant is closest — it delivers weekly summaries — but it's framed as a general trend/Q&A surface, not a single prioritized insight with one concrete recommended action. Mint's weekly summary, while proactively pushed, was purely descriptive (top categories/days), not prescriptive — this gap predates the current competitive set. This maps directly to what Nudge's own users asked for: "If I got one really useful insight a week I would open it every week" (research/nps-analysis.md, item #5) and "I need it to tell me what to do" (item #3).

2. **No competitor treats a stated savings goal as a recurring, proactive re-engagement touchpoint.** Monarch redesigned its Goals *structure* (save-up vs. debt-paydown) but that's a one-time setup improvement, not an ongoing pull-back mechanic. YNAB ties goals deeply into its budgeting methodology, but that requires the user to already be actively budgeting — it doesn't proactively re-engage a passive user. This is the exact gap a Nudge user flagged: "I set a savings goal but Nudge has never once mentioned it since. It is like it forgot." (research/nps-analysis.md, item #6).

Both gaps line up with the Engage v2 hypothesis already in [strategy.md](../strategy.md) — a personalized weekly insight tied to goal progress is not something any of these competitors, live or discontinued, currently own well.
