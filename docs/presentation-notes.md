# Speaker Notes — Engage v2 Quarterly Review

## Slide 1 — The Problem

Our 30-day retention has dropped from 44% to 37% over the last two quarters. That's the number I want everyone anchored on before anything else. The reason isn't mysterious — when we talked to users, the pattern was consistent: the app gives people a great first moment when they connect their account and see their spending breakdown, and then nothing happens after that. Nothing changes, nothing asks anything of them, and they drift away. That's the whole problem in one sentence, and everything else in this deck is about closing that specific gap.

## Slide 2 — Why Now

Since we last talked, we ran full discovery — user interviews and a competitive scan — and both landed on the exact same gap independently, which is the kind of convergence that makes me trust a finding. No competitor in this space currently owns a single personalized insight tied to an ongoing savings goal — that's real, unclaimed space, not just something we assume because we haven't looked outside. We also learned something that changes how urgent this is: the real, durable version of personalization — the kind that actually keeps someone like our power users around — takes about three months to build up. Users don't wait three months. They're gone well before that. So every quarter we don't act, we're not just losing the retention points we already lost — we're losing them on the same trend line, for a reason we now understand well enough to fix.

## Slide 3 — The Proposal

Here's what we're proposing to build: a personalized weekly money summary — the user's top spending insight for the week, compared to a benchmark, one clear action we suggest they take, and their savings-goal progress if they have one. It goes out as an email that pulls them back into an in-app screen. We're sequencing it so the savings-goal piece ships first, because that's the fastest piece we can validate. I want to be equally clear about what this isn't: we are not claiming this replicates the deep, three-month personalization I just mentioned — that's not achievable for a new user in week one, and we're not going to pretend otherwise. And to be direct about today's ask — I am not asking you to approve a company-wide rollout in this meeting. I'm asking for something narrower, which I'll get to.

## Slide 4 — Evidence

We built a working prototype and tested it against three user profiles drawn directly from our interviews, not hypothetical personas. I want to read you a couple of their actual words, because they explain the problem better than any chart I could build: one user told us "the app just showed me the same dashboard every time" and said he switched to a competitor because "it feels like it is asking something of me" — that's exactly the gap we're closing. Another said she'd been "waiting for Nudge to tell me the next step" while "nothing has changed since the first day." When we tested this in a small pilot, the numbers moved the way we hoped — a 14-point lift in 30-day retention, a 30-point lift at day 7, and an open rate on the summary that climbed instead of fading over four weeks. But I have to be straight with you: at the sample size we ran, that result is not yet statistically significant — the confidence interval actually includes zero. It's a real, promising signal. It is not proof yet, and I'm not going to present it to you as more certain than it is.

## Slide 5 — The Plan

Because that pilot wasn't conclusive, the plan is to run a properly powered test — about 1,160 users per group, roughly 2,320 total, which gives us enough data to actually trust the answer either way. How long that takes depends entirely on one decision: if we keep the test scoped to brand-new signups the way the pilot was, it takes about 27 weeks — too long. If we broaden it to our wider active-user base, it's about 5 weeks. That's a 22-week swing sitting on a single choice, and I need you to help me make that call. Two other things sit on the risk list: the ranking logic that decides which insight to surface still needs to be built, and — this one's bigger — we still haven't confirmed whether 30-day retention is actually the metric the company weighs this against long-term, versus just the metric this initiative happens to be using.

## Slide 6 — The Ask

So here's the actual ask today, and it's smaller than it might sound after everything I just walked through: approve running the properly powered test, not a full rollout — we're not there yet, and I don't want to oversell you into a decision the data can't support. And second, I need you to make the population-scope call, because that single decision determines whether we have an answer in five weeks or twenty-seven. Those are the two things I need to leave this room with today.
